    Consumer guide¶

    You want metered access to an API or RPC endpoint without a permanent static key. This guide walks through buying, using and reclaiming a lease with Stellar.

    The deal you are getting¶

    • You prepay rate_per_call × requested_calls into Soroban escrow.
    • You get an on-chain authorization that expires after duration_secs.
    • You pay only for the calls you use: unused balance is refunded at settlement.
    • If the provider never settles, you reclaim the entire deposit 24 hours after expiry — no support ticket, no trust required.

    Your Stellar account is your identity. There is no API key to copy into a .env file and no separate credential service.

    1. Pick a service¶

    stellar contract invoke --id $REGISTRY --source-account <funded-account> \
  --network testnet -- get_service --service_id 1

    Returns the provider, rate_per_call, is_active and endpoint_hash. Confirm the endpoint_hash matches the endpoint descriptor the provider published off-chain before you escrow anything.

    2. Fund your account¶

    Any SEP-41 token the registry is configured with can be escrowed. On Testnet the native asset's SAC is the usual choice, and Friendbot funds accounts with 10,000 fake XLM.

    # Testnet only: create + fund in one step
stellar keys generate consumer --network testnet --fund

# or fund an address you already have
curl "https://friendbot.stellar.org?addr=$(stellar keys address consumer)"

    3. Create the lease¶

    export REGISTRY=<your registry contract id>
export CONSUMER=$(stellar keys address consumer)

stellar contract invoke \
  --id $REGISTRY --source-account consumer --network testnet \
  --send=yes -- create_lease \
     --consumer $CONSUMER \
     --service_id 1 \
     --requested_calls 500 \
     --duration_secs 3600

    This escrows rate_per_call × 500 and prints the new lease_id. The transaction emits lease_created { lease_id, service_id, consumer, allocated_calls, expires_at, locked_deposit }; keep the transaction hash as proof of purchase.

    Choose your numbers deliberately. If your lease expires while you still have unused calls, those calls are not usable — remaining_calls drops to 0 at expires_at. The maximum duration is 30 days (MAX_LEASE_DURATION_SECS). Under-buy with a generous duration, then renew, rather than over-buying with a short one.

    Order of arguments matters. consumer is the first argument. This is the exact ABI; older documentation that shows service first is wrong.

    4. Use the access¶

    Tell the provider which lease backs your requests. Because the lease records your Stellar address, the provider authenticates you with a Stellar signature (SEP-10 challenge–response, or an equivalent signed challenge) and checks that you are lease.consumer. No bearer token is issued by KeyLease, and none is needed.

    Concretely, for a provider that follows Integration:

    1. complete the provider's Stellar challenge with your account,
    2. send requests with your lease_id (path, query or header, as the provider documents) or let the provider resolve it from your authenticated address,
    3. read x-keylease-calls-remaining if the provider exposes it.

    If you can sign for the account that owns the lease, you can use it — there is no separate credential to store or rotate. If you rotate that account, create a new lease from the new one.

    5. Watch the lease¶

    stellar contract invoke --id $REGISTRY --source-account consumer \
  --network testnet -- get_lease --lease_id 1

stellar contract invoke --id $REGISTRY --source-account consumer \
  --network testnet -- remaining_calls --lease_id 1

stellar contract invoke --id $REGISTRY --source-account consumer \
  --network testnet -- lease_status --lease_id 1

    lease_status is 0 (Active), 1 (Expired) or 2 (Settled).

    6. Get your money back¶

    Normal path — automatic. When the provider settles, the unused balance is transferred back to you in the same transaction. Nothing to claim.

    Provider went dark. After expires_at + 24 hours, reclaim everything:

    stellar contract invoke \
  --id $REGISTRY --source-account consumer --network testnet \
  --send=yes -- revoke_expired_lease --consumer $CONSUMER --lease_id 1

    This refunds the full locked_deposit and emits lease_revoked { lease_id, consumer, refund }. It works for a lease that was never used and for one that was partially used but never settled.

    Why you must wait 24 hours¶

    That window is reserved for the provider to settle legitimately. It is the provider's guarantee, and your refund is the counterweight. It is a fixed constant in the contract (SETTLE_GRACE_PERIOD_SECS = 86_400).

    Refund decision table¶

    Lease state What happens
    Provider settles before/at expiry You get locked_deposit − used × rate back
    Provider settles after expiry within 24h Same as above
    Provider settles with verified_calls = 0 You get the entire deposit back
    Provider never settles, now ≥ expiry + 24h You revoke and get the entire deposit
    Provider never settles, inside the 24h window Nothing yet — wait

    Costs¶

    Creating a lease and revoking one are two Soroban transactions (network fees plus the escrow transfer). There is no per-call on-chain cost — the provider settles with a single call count. Check the current fee with stellar network settings or your RPC provider.

    create_lease also pays storage rent for keeping the lease entry alive through its term, the 24-hour grace window and a one-day buffer. Rent depends on duration, not call count, so a short lease with a larger allotment is cheaper per call than a long lease with a tiny one. The network enforces a minimum persistent TTL (≈7 days on Testnet), so a very short lease still pays that floor.
