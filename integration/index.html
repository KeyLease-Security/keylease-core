    Integration¶

    KeyLease has no privileged off-chain service and no bespoke credential format. A provider integrates with the Stellar ledger directly: it verifies the lease on chain, binds the caller to the lease's consumer using Stellar account authentication, and settles through the contract.

    This page describes the supported pattern. Any implementation that follows it is compatible with the protocol.

    What the credential is¶

    The lease is the credential. get_lease(lease_id) returns the authoritative terms: allocated_calls, used_calls, expires_at, settled, and the consumer who owns the refund path. A provider admits a request only when that on-chain record says it is allowed.

    There is nothing else to issue: no API key, no session JWT, no signing secret. The consumer's identity is its Stellar address (G… or C…).

    The verification pipeline¶

    A provider should implement these steps, in order, and fail closed at every one:

    1. Authenticate the caller with Stellar. Use a challenge–response flow with the consumer's Stellar key; SEP-10 Stellar Web Authentication is the standard way to prove account ownership to an off-chain service. The result is a trusted Address for the request — not a password.
    2. Resolve the lease on chain. Call get_lease(lease_id) over Soroban RPC and cache the result briefly (a few seconds is usually enough for a busy provider). An unreachable RPC is a 503, never an allow.
    3. Check the lease, at ledger time. The lease must exist, must not be settled, and must not be expired. Compare expires_at against ledger time, not your server's clock: expires_at was produced by env.ledger().timestamp() inside the contract, so the two are different instruments. lease_status(lease_id) and remaining_calls(lease_id) make that comparison on-chain for you. Reject a settled or expired lease with 403, and reject before the request goes upstream — see Reject before you serve.
    4. Check ownership and service binding. The authenticated address must equal lease.consumer; that is what makes a stolen lease id worthless. It is not the whole check: lease.service_id must also equal the service this endpoint actually sells. A provider that runs more than one service and skips the second half admits traffic from the wrong pool — and because settle_lease resolves the price from lease.service_id, it is paid that service's rate for this service's work. consumer is mutable: the current owner can call transfer_lease, so treat a cached lease as stale when the address no longer matches and re-read it before answering 403.
    5. Enforce the quota. Use max(lease.used_calls, local_counter) against lease.allocated_calls; reject with 429 when exhausted, and increment the counter only for admitted requests.
    6. Serve the request, adding the remaining-calls header if you want the client to see its budget.
    7. Settle with settle_lease when the lease expires or when the allotment is fully consumed, then finalize with finalize_settlement after the 24-hour challenge window. Settlement only proposes the split; funds move at finalization. If the consumer asks to end the lease early (request_early_cancel), decide with approve_early_cancel (report served calls and release the unused balance) or reject_early_cancel (leave the lease running).

    Step 5's local counter is an optimisation, not the source of truth. On-chain allocated_calls × rate_per_call bounds what the provider can ever be paid, so a tampered counter can only make the provider charge less. It can never over-charge the consumer.

    Reject before you serve¶

    Steps 3–5 are the last point at which a call can be refused at no cost. Once the request has been forwarded you cannot un-spend it, and the two states that should have blocked it are precisely the two that make being paid for it impossible:

    • A settled lease has already released its escrow — payout + refund emptied locked_deposit — and a second settle_lease for it reverts with LeaseAlreadySettled (7). Every call you serve against it is unpaid, permanently.
    • An expired lease can still be settled by you, but the consumer can reclaim the entire deposit once the 24-hour grace window elapses, and a revoked lease is settled too: settle and revoke cannot both happen for one lease. Calls you serve after a revocation can never be reported.

    So the order in your handler is: authenticate, read, check, count — then forward. Make it testable as a property of the whole path rather than of the response: assert the upstream mock received zero calls for a settled lease and for an expired one, not merely that the client saw a 403.

    Ledger time, not clock time¶

    expires_at is a ledger timestamp. env.ledger().timestamp() is the network's own clock, advanced by consensus roughly every five seconds, and it is not your server's wall clock. Skew cuts both ways:

    Skew Symptom Who loses
    Your clock runs ahead of ledger time nothing — you are early, you reject a lease still valid on-chain consumer availability, your revenue
    Your clock runs behind you keep serving a lease that has already expired on-chain, and cannot settle for it you

    Two rules follow:

    1. Never let a client-supplied time be an input. A countdown in your UI may use the browser clock; the admission decision may not. If a caller can send a timestamp that changes whether you serve, you have replaced an on-chain check with a guess.
    2. Do not cache past the boundary. A cached lease must be re-read before its expires_at, and any lease within a tolerance window of expiry (a minute is plenty) should be re-read rather than trusted from cache. If you would rather not compare clocks at all, ask the contract: lease_status(lease_id) and remaining_calls(lease_id) evaluate expiry at ledger time for you, and a read costs one simulation with no signing key involved — and no rent, because reads never extend an entry's lifetime. Only writes and the two bump_* calls do; see Storage lifetime.

    Keep SEP-10's own timeout separate from lease expiry. The challenge is valid for minutes and is measured against your server clock; the lease is measured against the ledger. A fresh SEP-10 session against an expired lease is still a rejected request.

    Testable: build a fixture whose expires_at falls between two wall-clock values and assert your decision is the same with the container clock offset by ±5 minutes. A check that only passes when the host clock happens to be right is testing the host, not your code.

    Settlement must be safe to retry¶

    The contract already makes it so: settled is persisted before any token transfer, so a replayed settle_lease reverts with LeaseAlreadySettled (7) and a re-entrant token cannot settle a lease twice (test_reentrant_token_cannot_settle_a_lease_twice). Your side of the protocol has to be built to the same standard:

    • Treat revert-with-7 as success. If your settler broadcasts, the transaction lands, and the response never reaches you, the retry is the correct recovery — and if you log it as a failure you will page someone for a settlement that paid out fine.
    • Reconcile from the event, not from your input. Record payout and refund from lease_settled, whose lease_id is a topic you can filter on. The contract's numbers are the ones that moved tokens; yours are the ones you asked for.
    • Dedupe by lease_id. Nothing else identifies a settlement. Your counter, your queue and your revenue ledger all key on it, so a duplicate lease_created replay cannot make you settle twice or book the income twice.

    Testable, as two cases: replay the same settlement against a fixture and assert exactly one lease_settled event for that lease with the second attempt reverting with 7; then kill the settler between broadcast and confirmation and restart it, asserting the lease ends settled and your ledger holds one payout for it.

    Reading a lease¶

    With the Stellar CLI:

    stellar contract invoke --id $REGISTRY --source-account keylease-admin \
  --network testnet -- get_lease --lease_id 1

    With @stellar/stellar-sdk, simulate a get_lease invocation and decode the result:

    import {
  Contract, TransactionBuilder, Networks, Account,
  nativeToScVal, scValToNative, rpc,
} from '@stellar/stellar-sdk';

const server = new rpc.Server(process.env.KEYLEASE_RPC_URL!);
const contract = new Contract(process.env.KEYLEASE_CONTRACT_ID!);

export async function readLease(leaseId: bigint) {
  const source = new Account(
    'GAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAWHF',
    '0',
  );
  const tx = new TransactionBuilder(source, {
    fee: '100',
    networkPassphrase: Networks.TESTNET,
  })
    .addOperation(contract.call('get_lease', nativeToScVal(leaseId, { type: 'u64' })))
    .setTimeout(30)
    .build();

  const sim = await server.simulateTransaction(tx);
  if (!rpc.Api.isSimulationSuccess(sim) || !sim.result) {
    throw new Error('get_lease failed');
  }
  return scValToNative(sim.result.retval) as {
    lease_id: bigint;
    service_id: bigint;
    consumer: string;
    allocated_calls: number;
    used_calls: number;
    created_at: bigint;
    expires_at: bigint;
    locked_deposit: bigint;
    settled: boolean;
  };
}

    The read views require no authorization, so simulation is enough — a provider never needs a Stellar secret key to verify a lease. That is deliberate: compromising an enforcement tier does not compromise escrowed funds.

    Following the event stream¶

    For accounting, dashboards and settlement automation, subscribe to the contract's events instead of polling every lease:

    stellar events --id $REGISTRY --network testnet --start-ledger <ledger>
    Event Use
    lease_created Build a work queue of leases to settle; alert on new demand.
    lease_delegated Attribute a sponsored lease to the account that paid its fee; the lease itself is unchanged.
    lease_settled Reconcile payouts and refunds; close the lease in your ledger.
    lease_revoked A consumer reclaimed an expired lease; the provider earned nothing.
    service_registered / service_status_changed Keep a service catalogue in sync.
    contract_upgraded Detect a new Wasm version (version() also changes).

    An event's first topic is always the event name as a symbol; the fields marked #[topic] in contracts/registry/src/events.rs follow it, and every remaining field is carried in the payload map. Topics are what a subscriber filters on, so which side of that line a field falls on decides whether you can act on it without decoding.

    Event Use
    lease_created Build a work queue of leases to settle; alert on new demand.
    lease_settlement_proposed A settlement is waiting out the challenge window; schedule a finalize at challengeable_until.
    lease_challenged / lease_challenge_cancelled The consumer disputed (or withdrew a dispute of) the reported calls; the payout is frozen until resolved.
    lease_challenge_resolved The admin decided a dispute (upheld), immediately followed by the payout event.
    lease_settled Funds actually left escrow: reconcile payouts and refunds; close the lease in your ledger.
    lease_cancellation_requested A consumer wants to exit early; route the decision to the provider.
    lease_cancellation_rejected / lease_cancellation_withdrawn No cancellation: the lease continues and must still be settled.
    lease_cancelled Early exit paid out; reconcile payout and refund like lease_settled.
    lease_settled Reconcile payouts and refunds; close the lease in your ledger.
    lease_revoked A consumer reclaimed an expired lease; the provider earned nothing.
    service_registered / service_status_changed Keep a service catalogue in sync.
    contract_upgraded Detect a new Wasm version (version() also changes).

    Topics, payloads and filters¶

    The --topic filter takes base64-encoded ScVal segments, one per topic position, and matches only events with exactly that many topics unless the last segment is the ** wildcard. A * segment matches any single topic.

    Event Topics (in order) Payload-only fields Filter
    lease_created name, lease_id service_id, consumer, allocated_calls, expires_at, locked_deposit --topic "AAAADwAAAA1sZWFzZV9jcmVhdGVkAAAA,*"
    lease_settled name, lease_id provider, verified_calls, payout, refund --topic "AAAADwAAAA1sZWFzZV9zZXR0bGVkAAAA,*"
    lease_revoked name, lease_id consumer, refund --topic "AAAADwAAAA1sZWFzZV9yZXZva2VkAAAA,*"
    service_registered name, service_id provider, rate_per_call, endpoint_hash --topic "AAAADwAAABJzZXJ2aWNlX3JlZ2lzdGVyZWQAAA==,*"
    service_status_changed name, service_id provider, is_active --topic "AAAADwAAABZzZXJ2aWNlX3N0YXR1c19jaGFuZ2VkAAA=,*"
    contract_upgraded name only admin, new_wasm_hash --topic "AAAADwAAABFjb250cmFjdF91cGdyYWRlZAAAAA=="
    registry_initialized name only admin --topic "AAAADwAAABRyZWdpc3RyeV9pbml0aWFsaXplZA=="
    escrow_token_set name only admin, token --topic "AAAADwAAABBlc2Nyb3dfdG9rZW5fc2V0"

    Note that only five of the eight events carry an id topic at all: contract_upgraded, registry_initialized and escrow_token_set have no id topics, so they can only be narrowed by contract id, and a lease's service_id is payload-only — a per-service queue must decode the payload rather than filter. Regenerate any of these strings yourself when you need a different id, e.g. for lease 1:

    printf '{"symbol":"lease_created"}' | stellar xdr encode --type ScVal
# AAAADwAAAA1sZWFzZV9jcmVhdGVkAAAA
printf '{"u64":"1"}' | stellar xdr encode --type ScVal
# AAAABQAAAAAAAAAB
stellar events --id $REGISTRY --network testnet --start-ledger <ledger> \
  --topic "AAAADwAAAA1sZWFzZV9jcmVhdGVkAAAA,AAAABQAAAAAAAAAB"

    --output json and --output pretty decode names and fields for you, which is usually enough for a one-off check; the encoded filters matter when a subscriber needs the server to narrow the stream.

    Checking a filter against the live contract¶

    The table above is read out of this repository's source. The deployment is the other half of the ABI, and an upgrade can move a field from one side of the topics line to the other, so confirm the layout you are about to hard-code:

    stellar contract info interface --id $REGISTRY --network testnet

    The spec comes back as the #[contractevent] structs the SDK reconstructed from it, and #[topic] marks exactly the fields that occupy a topic position:

    #[soroban_sdk::contractevent(topics = ["lease_created"])]
pub struct LeaseCreated {
    #[topic]
    pub lease_id: u64,
    pub service_id: u64,
    // remaining fields are payload-only
}

    A field printed without #[topic] cannot be narrowed on at any ledger range; decode it instead. Run this after an upgrade that touched an event, and treat the printed struct as the ABI your subscribers see rather than the Rust source you merged — the two can disagree for one release.

    Filtering from your own code¶

    A subscriber that calls the RPC itself puts the same segments in a filter's topics field, which is an array of arrays: one inner array per topic position, with the same arity and * rules as --topic.

    {
  "startLedger": 5819050,
  "filters": [
    {
      "type": "contract",
      "contractIds": ["CCEZYHUJUICXVPLH6UC2OFZL2RG23QQ7EMZOWE32WVQPT25DCTIAFH62"],
      "topics": [["AAAADwAAAA1sZWFzZV9jcmVhdGVkAAAA", "*"]]
    }
  ]
}

    @stellar/stellar-sdk types it identically, and its request union keeps the two resume modes apart: startLedger (with optional endLedger and limit) or cursor (with limit), never both.

    // `persistedLedger` is your own resume point, not a value the RPC owes you
const { events, cursor, oldestLedger } = await server.getEvents({
  startLedger: persistedLedger,
  filters: [
    {
      type: "contract",
      contractIds: [process.env.KEYLEASE_CONTRACT_ID!],
      topics: [["AAAADwAAAA1sZWFzZV9jcmVhdGVkAAAA", "*"]],
    },
  ],
});

    Recipes¶

    • lease_created — open (or upsert) a lease record keyed by lease_id, storing service_id, consumer, allocated_calls, expires_at and locked_deposit from the payload, and push it onto the settlement queue ordered by expires_at. This is the only event that tells you escrow exists, so anything you do not record here must be re-read with get_lease.
    • lease_settled — close the lease in your ledger and reconcile: assert payout + refund == locked_deposit from the create event, and credit the provider payout. A payout of 0 means the provider refunded a lease it could not serve, which is worth alerting on.
    • lease_revoked — mark the lease closed with no revenue and record it against the provider's reliability. refund here is always the full locked_deposit. This event is also the only way to tell a revocation apart from a zero-call settlement, because both leave settled = true: see LeaseStatus and the settled flag.
    • service_status_changed — update the catalogue entry for service_id. is_active = false blocks new create_lease calls only, so stop quoting the service to new consumers while still serving and settling leases you already hold.
    • contract_upgraded — the running logic changed. Take new_wasm_hash from the payload, confirm version() reports the release you expected, and re-run the post-upgrade checks in Upgrading before trusting new leases. Reconcile any in-flight lease counters your indexer holds, because the payload carries no ids to scope this to.
    • service_registered — add the service to the catalogue with its rate_per_call and endpoint_hash; the hash is the commitment your enforcement layer compares the real endpoint against.
    • registry_initialized / escrow_token_set — deployment-lifecycle events. Treat escrow_token_set as a hard stop: the escrow asset changed, so the unit of every payout and refund you have been accumulating changed with it.

    Every id you can filter on is in the table above; event names, topics and payload fields are part of the ABI and are listed in the contract reference.

    Event schema version¶

    Every payload includes a schema_version key (u32), and the contract also publishes it as on-chain metadata under eventSchemaVersion. The number describes the event schema, not the release: version moves on every deploy, schema_version only when an event gains or loses a field. Fields are added and never removed, renamed or retyped, and topics never change, so an old decoder keeps working on new events.

    Branch on the payload key, not on the contract's version:

    Payload says What to do
    no schema_version key Revision 0: an event from before versioning was introduced. Its fields are today's fields minus schema_version, so decode it with the current parser and ignore the missing key.
    0 < schema_version <= 1 Decode the keys you know and ignore any extra key. A payload newer than your build within the same major revision stays readable by design.
    schema_version > 1 Your decoder is behind the deployment. Store the event verbatim, surface it as unhandled, and alert — do not guess at the shape of an escrow record. 
    version = payload.get("schema_version", 0)   # absent means pre-versioning
if version > SUPPORTED_SCHEMA_VERSION:
    quarantine(event)                        # never silently drop escrow

    Prefer the payload key over the eventSchemaVersion metadata when decoding history: metadata describes the Wasm running now, while an event from an old ledger was emitted by the schema that was running then. Read the metadata once at startup to learn the current version, and index from there.

    Resuming where you left off¶

    stellar events takes --start-ledger or --cursor, and a JSON-RPC subscriber takes the same pair. Persist whichever you used. Delivery is at-least-once after a restart, so key every projection row by lease_id or service_id and make the writes idempotent.

    Retention is the endpoint's, not yours, and it is a rolling window, so a resume from too far back is refused rather than answered with an empty list. The deploy ledger recorded in Deployments is 5382788; asking the public Testnet endpoint for it today returns:

    startLedger must be within the ledger range: 5698139 - 5819098

    Every response states the window it is serving, as oldestLedger and latestLedger with their close times, so an indexer does not have to guess when it is about to lose history. Measured on that same endpoint: 5698126 to 5819085, which is 120,959 ledgers spanning 604,791 seconds — seven days at five seconds a ledger.

    Three consequences:

    • Watch oldestLedger against your own cursor. The gap closes while you are still healthy, so alert on the trend rather than on the rejection you get once it has already lapped you.
    • A week of downtime is not backfillable from the ledger. Rebuild from the counters (service_count, lease_count) and the read views, continue from oldestLedger, and mark the range you cannot replay instead of treating it as empty.
    • Anything older than the window needs the explorer. The recorded history in Deployments is provable by transaction link, not by stellar events — and not by a read, either, once the entry's lifetime has run out; see Storage lifetime.

    Storage lifetime¶

    Lease and service entries are persistent entries with a bounded lifetime, and reading one no longer extends it. If you keep a long-lived projection of leases, you may want to keep the on-chain record alive alongside it:

    stellar contract invoke --id $REGISTRY --source-account indexer --network testnet \
  -- bump_lease --lease_id 1

    bump_lease and bump_service are permissionless; whoever calls them pays the rent for the extension. A bump can only lengthen a lifetime, never shorten it, and bump_lease rejects a settled lease, so there is nothing to grief with an entry whose funds have already moved. Settlement itself and create_lease keep the entries they touch alive on their own; you only need an explicit bump for state that is being read and not written for the length of its horizon.

    Runnable Event Indexer Example¶

    An example event indexer is provided in examples/indexer. It polls Soroban RPC getEvents for lease_created and lease_settled contract events, maintains a local file-based lease ledger (lease_ledger.json), and prints payouts and refunds when leases settle.

    Because indexing relies purely on reading historical and live RPC event logs, no secret keys or transaction signing are required—any read-only identity or anonymous client can run the indexer.

    Running against Testnet¶

    # Build the indexer
cargo build --manifest-path examples/indexer/Cargo.toml

# Run against Stellar Testnet starting from a recent ledger
cargo run --manifest-path examples/indexer/Cargo.toml -- \
  --rpc-url https://soroban-testnet.stellar.org \
  --contract-id <REGISTRY_CONTRACT_ID> \
  --start-ledger <LEDGER_SEQUENCE> \
  --ledger-file lease_ledger.json

    Options:

    • --rpc-url <URL>: Soroban RPC endpoint (default: https://soroban-testnet.stellar.org).
    • --contract-id <ID>: The KeyLease registry contract address (C...).
    • --start-ledger <N>: Ledger sequence number to start indexing from.
    • --ledger-file <PATH>: Path to persist the JSON lease ledger (default: lease_ledger.json).
    • --poll-interval <SECS>: Interval between RPC polls in seconds (default: 5).
    • --once: Run a single poll cycle and exit.
    • --fixture <PATH>: Offline mode: replay a JSON array of getEvents event responses without network access.

    Topic Layout and Filtering¶

    The indexer matches on the contract's real event structure:

    • Topic 0: Event name symbol (lease_created or lease_settled).
    • Topic 1: Lease identifier (u64).
    • Value: Event data map containing:
    • lease_created: service_id, consumer, allocated_calls, expires_at, locked_deposit.
    • lease_settled: provider, verified_calls, payout, refund.

    When a lease_settled event is observed, the indexer reconciles the stored record, updates its status to Settled, and outputs the recorded payout and refund amounts.

    Settling¶

    Settlement is two transactions. The provider proposes a split at or after expiry, or as soon as every allocated call is consumed:

    stellar contract invoke --id $REGISTRY --source-account provider --network testnet \
  --send=yes -- settle_lease \
    --provider $PROVIDER --lease_id 1 --verified_calls 320

    That transaction moves no funds. It emits lease_settlement_proposed and opens a 24-hour consumer challenge window. Once the window closes, anyone can release the funds:

    stellar contract invoke --id $REGISTRY --source-account provider --network testnet \
  --send=yes -- finalize_settlement --lease_id 1

    Reporting verified_calls = 0 proposes a full refund for a lease you could not serve. Choose a settlement cadence that fits your traffic: one settlement per lease, not per request.

    The consumer challenge window¶

    The consumer can dispute the provider's reported call count while the window is open:

    stellar contract invoke --id $REGISTRY --source-account consumer --network testnet \
  --send=yes -- challenge_settlement \
    --consumer $CONSUMER --lease_id 1 \
    --evidence_hash <sha256-of-your-evidence>

    Only a 32-byte commitment goes on chain; the evidence itself is presented to the admin off chain. A challenge freezes the payout. The consumer may withdraw it (cancel_challenge) and the settlement returns to pending, with the original deadline unchanged. The registry admin resolves an active challenge on chain:

    stellar contract invoke --id $REGISTRY --source-account admin --network testnet \
  --send=yes -- resolve_challenge --admin $ADMIN --lease_id 1 --upheld true

    upheld = true refunds the entire deposit to the consumer and pays the provider nothing. upheld = false releases the proposal unchanged. Index upheld from the lease_challenge_resolved event and reconcile the following lease_settled event.

    Hardening checklist¶

    • [ ] Verify leases server-side; never trust a lease_id supplied by the client without checking consumer against an authenticated Stellar address.
    • [ ] Fail closed on RPC errors, malformed lease data and unknown leases.
    • [ ] Cache lease reads briefly (≈5–30 s) but never past the request you are authorising; re-read on a cache miss or when close to expires_at.
    • [ ] Share the per-lease counter if you run more than one instance, or accept that each instance can grant the full allotment (the provider only loses revenue; the consumer is still bounded on chain).
    • [ ] Keep provider signing keys out of the request path where possible: reads need no key, only settlement does.
    • [ ] Watch lease_created so no lease expires unsettled and becomes revocable.
    • [ ] Bind the lease to the service it is being redeemed against: reject unless lease.service_id is the service this endpoint sells. Test: a lease bought for service A, presented to endpoint B, gets 403 and forwards nothing upstream.
    • [ ] Take expiry from ledger time, never from the host clock or a client timestamp, and re-read any cached lease before its expires_at. Test: the same fixture gives the same verdict with the container clock offset by ±5 minutes.
    • [ ] Treat settlement as retry-safe: retry on any error, read LeaseAlreadySettled (7) as success, and book revenue from the lease_settled event keyed by lease_id. Test: settling the same lease twice leaves exactly one payout in your ledger and one event on-chain.
    • [ ] Reject Settled and Expired leases before forwarding the request, not after answering it. Test: the upstream mock records zero calls for a settled lease and for an expired one.
    • [ ] Never log Stellar secret seeds.
