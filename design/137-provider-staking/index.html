    Design note — Provider staking and slashing (#137)¶

    Status: Proposed — awaiting maintainer approval. No contract code is changed by this note. Per CONTRIBUTING, a maintainer must approve this design before implementation begins.

    Issue: #137 — Add provider staking and slashing for proven over-reporting Target release: v0.3.0 · Touches: lib.rs, storage.rs, types.rs, events.rs, errors.rs

    1. Problem statement¶

    settle_lease trusts the provider's verified_calls. The allotment caps the damage per lease, and the consumer can reclaim an unsettled deposit after the grace window, but a provider that reports more calls than it actually served keeps the over-payment. The only penalty is that the consumer does not come back. There is no on-chain cost to over-reporting within allocated_calls, so the protocol's central trust assumption — "the provider reports honestly" — has no economic backstop.

    This note proposes an opt-in provider stake that a service must post before it accepts leases, plus a bounded, evidence-based slashing process for proven over-reporting.

    Goals¶

    • Give consumers a recourse that is funded by the provider, not by other users.
    • Make the penalty proportional to the over-report and hard-capped, so the mechanism cannot be used to confiscate a provider's business.
    • Keep staking opt-in: existing services and deployments keep working unchanged, and unstaked services continue to be leaseable.
    • Never delay, block or alter settlement or the consumer refund path.
    • Add no unbounded loops, no new trust in off-chain data, and no change to the existing Service/Lease storage layout.

    Non-goals¶

    • Proving real HTTP usage on-chain. The contract cannot observe traffic; the design constrains who may accuse, how much may be slashed and where the money goes, and relies on a public evidence hash plus an arbiter.
    • Insuring consumers for non-delivery. That remains the refund path plus, now, a stake.
    • A general arbitration court, appeals beyond one challenge round, or slashing for anything other than over-reporting.

    2. Proposed architecture¶

    2.1 Stake custody¶

    Stake is held by the registry in the same SEP-41 token as escrow, but accounted for separately. Escrow accounting is per lease (locked_deposit); stake accounting is per provider. The contract must never infer one from the token balance — sum(locked deposits) + sum(stakes) == token balance is an invariant the tests assert; the token balance itself is never used as a source of truth.

    Stake is associated with the provider address (Service.provider), not the service id. One pool covers every service that provider opts in, which avoids duplicating capital across services and keeps the slash/withdraw bookkeeping bounded. Revenue-split recipients (#133) are payees only; they are not stakers and cannot be slashed.

    2.2 Opt-in per service¶

    A provider opts a service into staking with opt_in_service_stake. The opt-in is a separate storage key (ServiceStake(service_id)) rather than a new field on Service, so existing Service entries keep decoding and the storage layout is upgrade-safe. Opting out only blocks new leases.

    create_lease enforces the invariant for opted-in services:

    if service is staked:
    provider stake must exist and available_stake >= min_stake
    the provider must not have a withdrawal pending

    If either check fails the lease reverts (InsufficientStake / StakeWithdrawalPending). Existing services that never opt in are unaffected; no new lease on an unstaked service ever requires stake.

    2.3 Minimum and configurable requirements¶

    The admin configures a single StakeConfig in instance storage:

    Field Meaning Default Bound
    min_stake Minimum available stake an opted-in service needs at lease creation — > 0
    dispute_bond Bond an accuser posts to open a dispute — > 0
    dispute_window_secs How long after a lease's dispute anchor a dispute may be opened — 1 h – 30 days
    challenge_period_secs Provider response window before resolution — 1 h – 7 days
    unbonding_period_secs Delay between withdrawal request and release — >= dispute_window + challenge_period, max 30 days
    max_slash_bps Hard cap on the share of a provider's stake one dispute may slash — 1 – 10 000 bps

    Staking is disabled until the admin sets a config; opt_in_service_stake and open_dispute revert with StakeNotConfigured before then. This means an upgraded deployment behaves exactly like today until the operator opts the protocol in. The admin can raise/lower min_stake later; lowering it takes effect immediately for new leases, raising it never invalidates existing leases.

    2.4 Evidence and slashing authority¶

    The contract cannot validate usage data, so "proven" is defined procedurally. A dispute may only be opened by the lease's consumer — the party that paid and therefore has standing — and only against a settled lease of a staked service, within the dispute window.

    Admissible evidence. The contract stores only a 32-byte commitment (evidence_hash) and emits it. The evidence bundle is published off-chain in a documented format so any third party can reproduce the verdict:

    • the lease id, service id and settlement transaction hash,
    • the provider's reported verified_calls,
    • per-call records the provider signed or that the consumer can verify (signed request logs, provider receipts, or the provider's own metering export),
    • a manifest hash over those artifacts, which is the evidence_hash.

    The provider may answer once with contest_dispute, supplying its own counter_evidence_hash. Both hashes and the final verdict are permanent event data.

    Who may slash.

    1. The consumer opens the dispute and posts dispute_bond.
    2. The provider may contest during challenge_period_secs.
    3. The arbiter — a dedicated role, defaulting to the admin and rotatable by the admin — resolves after the challenge period with resolve_dispute.
    4. Only then, and only if approved, is stake moved.

    Bounds that prevent arbitrary confiscation.

    • Only a consumer with a settled lease can accuse; the arbiter cannot open a dispute against a provider it dislikes.
    • The disputed amount is bounded by the on-chain over-report: overreported_calls * rate_per_call, where overreported_calls <= lease.allocated_calls.
    • The slash is additionally capped at stake_total * max_slash_bps / 10_000 and can never exceed the provider's remaining stake.
    • Slashed value is paid to the accusing consumer, never to the admin, the arbiter or the registry. The arbiter therefore cannot profit from a ruling.
    • A rejected dispute returns nothing to the accuser: the bond is paid to the provider as compensation for the false accusation, and the provider's stake is untouched.
    • Every step emits an event with the evidence hashes and amounts; the arbiter role and config are admin-visible and rotatable.

    A single challenge round is deliberately the whole procedure. It is a bounded governance process, not a court: the cap, the consumer-only standing and the "proceeds to the accuser" rule are what make it safe to keep small.

    2.5 Withdrawal rules¶

    Stake leaves in two steps:

    1. request_stake_withdrawal(provider, amount) requires:
    2. the provider has opted out of every service (staked_services == 0),
    3. no unsettled leases are attributable to the provider (open_leases == 0),
    4. no dispute against the provider is pending (pending_disputes == 0),
    5. amount <= available, and amount > 0. It moves amount from available to unbonding and sets unbonding_until = now + unbonding_period_secs. A withdrawal request also makes create_lease revert with StakeWithdrawalPending for that provider's services, so no new exposure can be created while capital is leaving.
    6. withdraw_stake(provider) requires now >= unbonding_until, open_leases == 0 and pending_disputes == 0, then transfers the unbonding amount to the provider.

    Because unbonding_period_secs >= dispute_window_secs + challenge_period_secs, every settlement that could still be disputed has either been disputed or is past its window before the capital is released, and unbonding stake remains slashable until the transfer happens. A new deposit_stake during unbonding cancels the request (unbonding returns to available, the timer clears), which is safe because the funds never left the contract.

    pending_disputes and open_leases are per-provider counters, incremented and decremented on the paths that create and close them, so withdrawal never scans a collection.

    2.6 Settlement and service lifecycle¶

    • Settlement and revocation are unchanged: stake never gates settle_lease or revoke_expired_lease, and the consumer's exit remains guaranteed. A provider that has been slashed can still settle leases it already sold.
    • Slashing is paid from the provider's stake, not from lease escrow, so payout + refund == locked_deposit still holds for every lease.
    • If a slash drops available below min_stake, the service is effectively deactivated without writing to Service: the next create_lease reverts InsufficientStake. This avoids iterating a provider's services to flip is_active, keeps Service layout-compatible, and makes topping the stake up an immediate recovery. set_service_active(false) remains available for an explicit pause, and a provider can always opt out.
    • A provider's opt-in/opt-out never changes the terms of an existing lease; those leases settle under the rules in force when they were created.

    3. Storage changes¶

    All additions are new DataKey variants. Service, Lease, LeaseStatus and every existing key keep their exact wire encoding.

    Key Storage Value TTL policy
    StakeConfig instance StakeConfig instance TTL, bumped on every state change
    Arbiter instance Address instance TTL
    Stake(Address) persistent StakeAccount extended on read/write, 30-day horizon
    ServiceStake(u64) persistent bool extended with the service, 30-day horizon
    DisputeCount instance u64 instance TTL
    Dispute(u64) persistent Dispute extended on read/write; retained through resolution + 30 days 
    pub struct StakeAccount {
    pub available: i128,
    pub unbonding: i128,
    pub unbonding_until: u64,
    pub open_leases: u32,      // leases of this provider not yet settled/revoked
    pub staked_services: u32,  // services currently opted in
    pub pending_disputes: u32, // disputes not yet resolved
}

pub struct Dispute {
    pub dispute_id: u64,
    pub lease_id: u64,
    pub service_id: u64,
    pub accuser: Address,       // lease.consumer
    pub provider: Address,      // service.provider
    pub evidence_hash: BytesN<32>,
    pub counter_evidence_hash: BytesN<32>, // zeroed until contested
    pub overreported_calls: u32,
    pub slash_amount: i128,     // computed and frozen at open time
    pub bond: i128,
    pub opened_at: u64,
    pub status: DisputeStatus,  // Open = 0, Contested = 1, Approved = 2, Rejected = 3
}

pub struct StakeConfig {
    pub min_stake: i128,
    pub dispute_bond: i128,
    pub dispute_window_secs: u64,
    pub challenge_period_secs: u64,
    pub unbonding_period_secs: u64,
    pub max_slash_bps: u32,
}

    slash_amount is frozen at open_dispute so topping up the stake, or a second dispute, cannot change what the first one may take. The bounded cap is re-evaluated against the balance at resolution and the payout is the smaller of the frozen amount and the remaining stake.

    4. Public interface changes¶

    Entry points¶

    Entry point Auth Description
    set_stake_config(admin, config) admin Set/update the staking configuration. Emits stake_config_set.
    set_arbiter(admin, arbiter) admin Rotate the dispute arbiter. Emits arbiter_set.
    deposit_stake(provider, amount) provider Transfer amount from the provider into the stake pool; cancels a pending withdrawal. Emits stake_deposited.
    request_stake_withdrawal(provider, amount) provider Start unbonding. Emits stake_withdrawal_requested.
    cancel_stake_withdrawal(provider) provider Return unbonding to available before release. Emits stake_withdrawal_cancelled.
    withdraw_stake(provider) provider Release unbonding stake after the delay. Emits stake_withdrawn.
    opt_in_service_stake(provider, service_id) provider Require stake for new leases of the service. Emits service_stake_changed.
    opt_out_service_stake(provider, service_id) provider Stop requiring stake for new leases. Emits service_stake_changed.
    open_dispute(consumer, lease_id, evidence_hash, overreported_calls) consumer Post the bond and open a dispute on a settled lease. Emits dispute_opened.
    contest_dispute(provider, dispute_id, counter_evidence_hash) provider Answer once inside the challenge period. Emits dispute_contested.
    resolve_dispute(arbiter, dispute_id, approve: bool) arbiter After the challenge period, slash or reject. Emits dispute_resolved and provider_slashed.
    get_stake(provider) — Result<StakeAccount, Error>
    get_stake_config() — Result<StakeConfig, Error>
    get_dispute(dispute_id) — Result<Dispute, Error>
    is_service_staked(service_id) — bool
    stake_requirement(service_id) — Result<Option<i128>, Error>: min_stake when staked, otherwise None

    Events¶

    Event Topics Data
    stake_config_set — admin, min_stake, dispute_bond, dispute_window_secs, challenge_period_secs, unbonding_period_secs, max_slash_bps
    arbiter_set — admin, arbiter
    stake_deposited provider amount, available, unbonding
    stake_withdrawal_requested provider amount, unbonding_until
    stake_withdrawal_cancelled provider amount
    stake_withdrawn provider amount, available
    service_stake_changed service_id provider, staked
    dispute_opened dispute_id lease_id, service_id, accuser, evidence_hash, overreported_calls, slash_amount, bond
    dispute_contested dispute_id provider, counter_evidence_hash
    dispute_resolved dispute_id approved, slash_amount, bond_to_provider, evidence_hash
    provider_slashed provider amount, remaining_available, remaining_unbonding

    service_registered, service_status_changed, lease_created, lease_settled and lease_revoked keep their exact payloads; the stake checks add no field to a lease.

    Error codes (appended, never renumbered)¶

    Code Variant Meaning
    17 StakeNotConfigured Staking has not been enabled by the admin
    18 InvalidStakeAmount Zero/negative stake, bond or config value
    19 InsufficientStake Provider's available stake is below min_stake
    20 StakeWithdrawalPending Withdrawal unbonding; no new leases
    21 WithdrawalNotReady Unbonding period has not elapsed
    22 PendingDispute A pending dispute blocks the action
    23 ServiceNotStaked Dispute against a service that did not opt in
    24 ServiceAlreadyStaked Opt-in on an already-staked service
    25 OutstandingLeases Unsettled leases block withdrawal
    26 NoStake No stake account for the provider
    27 DisputeWindowClosed Dispute opened too late (or lease not settled)
    28 ChallengePeriodActive Resolution attempted before the provider's window closed
    29 DisputeClosed Dispute already resolved
    30 InvalidDispute Wrong accuser, over-report bound, or duplicate contest
    31 InvalidStakeConfig Config outside the documented bounds
    32 NothingToWithdraw No unbonding stake to release

    5. Authorization and governance model¶

    Role Can do Cannot do
    Provider Deposit, opt in/out, request/cancel/complete withdrawal, contest a dispute, settle its leases Slash, resolve disputes, withdraw while exposed
    Consumer Open a dispute on its settled lease after posting the bond Open disputes on others' leases, slash directly
    Arbiter Resolve a dispute after the challenge period Open disputes, change config, touch escrow, keep slashed value
    Admin Set config, rotate the arbiter, upgrade the Wasm Slash, resolve disputes, move stake or escrow

    The admin and arbiter should be separate keys (both cold/multisig) in production; the default of "arbiter = admin" exists so a fresh deployment works without a second key, and the role is rotatable and announced by arbiter_set. Governance is deliberately narrow: the only discretionary decision in the protocol is the one-bit approve in resolve_dispute, and its consequences are capped, recipient-fixed and permanently logged.

    6. Backward compatibility¶

    • Storage: only new keys. Service and Lease are untouched, so a v0.3.0 Wasm can be installed over a v0.2.x deployment with live services and leases.
    • Existing services: remain unstaked and continue to accept leases with no stake requirement. Nothing becomes mandatory.
    • Existing leases: unchanged terms; settlement, refund and revocation do not consult stake.
    • ABI: all new entry points, events and error codes are additive. No error number is reused and no event payload changes.
    • Default state: staking is inert until set_stake_config is called, so an upgraded deployment behaves identically to v0.2.x.
    • Events: the existing event stream for lease lifecycle is byte-identical; stake and dispute events are new names.

    7. Security considerations¶

    Re-entrancy and ordering. Every money path follows checks-effects-interactions. open_dispute persists the dispute and the bond before transferFrom-style movement (the consumer transfer happens after the counter and bond are recorded). resolve_dispute sets the dispute to Approved/Rejected, decrements pending_disputes and debits stake before any token transfer, so a malicious recipient contract cannot re-enter for a second slash or a second bond refund. withdraw_stake zeroes the unbonding balance before the transfer. A re-entrant token would see terminal state and revert the whole transaction.

    Storage integrity. open_leases and pending_disputes are counters, not collections, and are maintained on both sides of each transition (create/settle/revoke, open/resolve). The tests assert they return to zero after a full lifecycle. Dispute records are immutable after resolution except for status. All persistent additions carry explicit TTL extensions; a stake entry is read/written on every path that needs it, and a dispute entry is extended when opened, contested and resolved.

    Economic attack vectors.

    Attack Mitigation
    Provider over-reports and withdraws before a dispute Unbonding ≥ dispute window + challenge period; unbonding remains slashable; withdrawal requires zero open leases and zero pending disputes; new leases are blocked while unbonding
    Consumer grieves a provider with disputes Bond forfeited to the provider on rejection; bounded cap; one contest round; evidence hash makes frivolous claims auditable
    Arbiter colludes with a consumer Slash proceeds go to the consumer, not the arbiter; per-dispute cap; admin-rotatable role; every ruling is event-logged with evidence hashes
    Provider keeps too little stake relative to revenue min_stake is admin-set and enforced on every new lease of a staked service; raising it never invalidates existing leases
    Slash is used to seize a business Only settled leases, consumer standing, over-report bound, max_slash_bps cap, one round, provider keeps all un-slashed stake and future revenue
    Double slash on one lease At most one dispute per lease (open_dispute rejects a lease that already has one, checked through a per-lease dispute index), and a terminal status
    Stake/escrow accounting confusion Separate internal ledgers; the token balance is never read as a source of truth; tests assert escrow + stake == balance
    Griefing by never contesting Resolution is permissionless in time but arbiter-driven; an uncontested dispute resolves on schedule

    Governance and upgrade implications. StakeConfig, Arbiter and the new keys must keep their meaning across upgrades; the implementation must never renumber errors or change the Service/Lease encoding. A malicious upgrade can already change any rule, so the arbiter/admin keys deserve the same cold-storage treatment as the existing admin key. Because min_stake and max_slash_bps are live parameters, changes to them should be announced and are visible in stake_config_set.

    Resource limits. Every new path is O(1) in storage reads/writes and does a constant number of token calls (at most two transfers in a resolution). No entry point iterates a user-controlled collection; recipient and service lists are never walked to enforce stake.

    8. Testing strategy¶

    New tests in contracts/registry/src/test.rs (plus the existing suite kept green):

    Stake lifecycle

    • deposit credits available, moves tokens, emits stake_deposited, extends TTL;
    • deposit with zero/negative amount reverts InvalidStakeAmount;
    • opt-in requires a config and an account; opt-in/opt-out emit service_stake_changed;
    • create_lease on a staked service reverts InsufficientStake below min_stake and succeeds at exactly min_stake;
    • unstaked services keep leasing with no stake at all (backward compatibility).

    Withdrawal

    • request moves available to unbonding, blocks new leases (StakeWithdrawalPending);
    • early withdraw_stake reverts WithdrawalNotReady;
    • withdraw succeeds after the delay and only then moves tokens;
    • request reverts while a lease is unsettled (OutstandingLeases), while a service is still opted in, and while a dispute is pending (PendingDispute);
    • deposit during unbonding cancels the request and restores available.

    Disputes and slashing

    • only the lease consumer can open (InvalidDispute / Unauthorized);
    • a dispute on an unstaked service reverts ServiceNotStaked;
    • a dispute on an unsettled or out-of-window lease reverts DisputeWindowClosed;
    • the bond is transferred in and the frozen slash_amount respects both the over-report bound and max_slash_bps;
    • contest is provider-only and single-shot; resolution before the challenge period reverts ChallengePeriodActive;
    • approval pays the accuser the slash plus the returned bond, debits stake (available first, then unbonding), emits dispute_resolved and provider_slashed;
    • rejection pays the bond to the provider, leaves stake untouched, and clears the pending counter;
    • double resolution reverts DisputeClosed; a second dispute on the same lease reverts;
    • a re-entrant token cannot double-slash or double-refund a bond.

    Settlement and lifecycle after a slash

    • an existing lease still settles and refunds correctly after its provider is slashed;
    • create_lease reverts InsufficientStake after the stake falls below min_stake (effective deactivation), and works again after a top-up;
    • opt_out_service_stake blocks new leases but leaves existing leases settleable;
    • payout + refund == locked_deposit and escrow + stake == token balance hold across the whole flow;
    • the real-SAC lifecycle test and the existing re-entrancy test still pass.

    9. Open questions and trade-offs¶

    1. Provider-level vs per-service stake. A single pool is capital-efficient and keeps checks O(1); a per-service pool isolates a bad service from a provider's other business. This note chooses provider-level and lets the provider opt out individual services. Rejected alternative: a stake field on Service (breaks stored-value layout) and a per-service pool (fragments capital and needs per-service withdrawal bookkeeping).
    2. Arbiter discretion. One challenge round with a capped, accuser-directed payout is cheap and bounded, but a corrupt arbiter can still approve a malicious dispute up to the cap. Alternatives: two-of-two (arbiter + admin) approval, a bonded arbiter, or a consumer/provider optimistic game with a fraud proof. Each adds interface and governance surface; we should decide before implementation whether disputes above a threshold need two approvals.
    3. Exposure-based stake. The design requires available >= min_stake, not "stake proportional to open escrow". Per-lease stake would price risk better but needs mid-lease top-ups and makes settlement conditional on stake. Question for maintainers: should a later version require min_stake >= k * open_leases?
    4. Dispute window anchor. Disputes are anchored at expires_at + grace + dispute_window, because Lease has no settled_at field and adding one would change its encoding. This grants a generous window to fully-consumed leases settled early. Alternative: add SettlementTime(lease_id) as a new key and anchor on the real settlement time.
    5. Bond size. A flat dispute_bond is simple but may be too small for large services or too large for small ones. Alternative: a bps-of-disputed-value bond with a floor and a ceiling.
    6. Who audits evidence. The on-chain record is a hash plus a verdict. If maintainers want verifiable evidence handling, the off-chain bundle format needs a documented schema and a canonical hashing rule before release.
    7. Unbonding UX. Requiring open_leases == 0 and full opt-out before a withdrawal request is conservative and can lock capital for up to a lease term plus the delay. Alternative: allow withdrawal requests with open leases by reserving the newest leases' exposure.
    8. Versioning. This is a breaking-in-behaviour (not ABI) change once a config is set; the workspace version should move to 0.3.0 and the release notes must describe the governance assumptions.
